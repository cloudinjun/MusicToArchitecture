# Decision 0001 — Primary Typology Shortlist

- Status: active shortlist; final selection pending
- Date: 2026-08-26
- Decision owner: user
- Career-value tags: V1, V2, V4

## Decision

The first implementation will select one primary typology from exactly three
candidates:

1. Library
2. Theater
3. Museum

No additional typology should enter the first selection round. The compiler should
not implement all three before the selection. Each candidate may receive a compact
constitution/program study sufficient to make an evidence-based choice.

## Why this decision exists

The primary typology needs enough architectural structure to test shared design
intent across program, massing, circulation, structure, facade, and interior while
remaining feasible as a complete portfolio project.

The selection must support the charter's career evidence:

- V1: formalize qualitative intent and typology invariants;
- V2: coordinate multiple building systems;
- V4: define visible architectural and compositional quality gates.

## Candidate hypotheses

These are starting hypotheses for comparison, not final judgments.

### Library

Potential strengths:

- clear gradients between public, quiet, social, staff, archive, and service zones;
- repetition and variation can operate through shelving, reading modules, bays, and
  daylight rhythms;
- multiple circulation speeds and thresholds can express continuity and interruption;
- manageable technical scope for an MVP.

Risks to test:

- the program can become spatially generic;
- musical influence may collapse into facade or shelving rhythm;
- a convincing climax/hierarchy needs deliberate architectural definition.

### Theater

Potential strengths:

- strong temporal sequence from arrival through foyer, house, performance, and exit;
- explicit front-of-house/back-of-house separation;
- structure, sightlines, circulation, facade, and interior can reveal coordinated
  intent;
- music has an authentic relationship to performance without requiring literal form.

Risks to test:

- acoustics, egress, sightlines, rigging, and long spans can expand scope quickly;
- the music connection may appear predetermined;
- professional acoustic claims must stay within implemented evidence.

### Museum

Potential strengths:

- sequence, pause, threshold, hierarchy, light, repetition, and climax are naturally
  legible;
- gallery flexibility allows controlled score variation;
- program, circulation, structure, facade, and interior can share one compositional
  model;
- strong fit for before/after and change-propagation evidence.

Risks to test:

- loose gallery requirements can make constraint validation appear weak;
- the result may resemble a generic form-generation project;
- score effects need to remain distinguishable from curatorial or stylistic choices.

## Selection criteria

Score each candidate only after defining a comparable minimal program and site
assumption.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Career evidence | Does the typology make V1–V4 visible to a recruiter? |
| Cross-system coordination | Can one intent be read across at least program, circulation, structure, and facade/interior? |
| Typology invariants | Are there meaningful requirements that music and style cannot override? |
| Controlled musical variation | Can two music inputs cause expected differences while invariants remain stable? |
| Failure evidence | Can the system produce and recover from credible adjacency, circulation, span, or envelope failures? |
| MVP feasibility | Can one complete architectural result be delivered at portfolio depth? |
| Evaluation clarity | Can quality gates be expressed without unsupported professional claims? |
| Portfolio legibility | Can a reviewer understand the problem and evidence quickly? |

Use an explicit 1–5 rating plus written evidence for each criterion. Do not select by
total score alone; record any disqualifying risk and the user's final judgment.

## Required artifacts before final selection

For each candidate, produce only:

- a one-paragraph typology constitution;
- a minimal program list and adjacency sketch;
- three to five hard invariants;
- one example of a shared score interpreted across multiple systems;
- one credible failure-and-recovery scenario;
- a rough scope/risk estimate.

These studies should stay diagrammatic. Do not build three generators.

## Consequences

- The previous museum-first recommendation is now one option in a three-candidate
  shortlist.
- Fire station is no longer committed as the transfer case.
- A transfer typology will be selected after the primary typology and compiler boundary
  are stable.
- Core schema design may proceed if it remains typology-neutral and does not encode a
  hidden preference for library, theater, or museum.
