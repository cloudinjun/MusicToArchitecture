# 07 — Deconstructivism-informed Controlled-fragmentation Facade Grammar

## Scope and research basis

MoMA's 1988 exhibition brought together architects whose work disturbed familiar
geometries through fragmentation, collision, skew, instability and displaced systems.
Their approaches remain distinct. This project grammar uses controlled transformations
and rule conflict while protecting program, circulation, constructability and envelope
continuity.

1. Peter Eisenman, **Wexner Center for the Arts**, 1983–1989.

## Facade thesis

The facade records a controlled conflict among two or more systems: grids, planes,
volumes, historical traces, program zones or circulation vectors. Operations such as
split, shear, rotate, displace, crop and collide create apparent instability while
actual support, use and weather enclosure remain traceable.

## Invariants

- `DC-INV-01` — every transformation identifies its source geometry, operation and
  magnitude.
- `DC-INV-02` — at least two coherent source systems precede their collision or
  fragmentation.
- `DC-INV-03` — primary program, required circulation and structural support survive
  transformations.
- `DC-INV-04` — facade fragments retain a continuous conceptual air/water barrier behind
  or through their joints.
- `DC-INV-05` — instability is perceptual; loose, unsupported or unmaintainable pieces
  fail.
- `DC-INV-06` — exceptions remain bounded enough that the source systems can be read.

## Legal variables and starting ranges

Project-authored experiment defaults:

| Parameter | Starting range | Owner and use |
|---|---:|---|
| plane rotation | 3–18° | grammar/score; structural clamp |
| grid shear | 0.05–0.30 bay per storey | grammar |
| fragment displacement | 0.05–0.40 bay | grammar/score |
| transformed facade share | 15–45% | grammar/human |
| source systems in conflict | 2–3 | grammar |
| major collision zones | 1–4 per building | program/sequence |
| residual panel/sliver minimum | material system limit; placeholder ≥ 0.3 m | fabrication |

## Forbidden operations

- unseeded random vertex movement or noise fields;
- transforming a facade before defining source grids/planes;
- letting fragments cross entrances, egress paths or protected program volumes;
- merging all fragments into one mesh with lost source/operation metadata;
- using sharp geometry while leaving panelization, joints and support unexamined;
- equating smooth continuous parametric differentiation with this grammar.

## Shared Score channels

| Dimension | Mapping in this grammar |
|---|---|
| Hierarchy | concentrate collision or displacement around a primary public sequence |
| Repetition | repeat the source grid/plane before selected disruptions |
| Variation | vary operation magnitude within one transformation family |
| Density | alter number of fragments or overlapping traces in bounded zones |
| Continuity | preserve a datum/route/barrier across displaced pieces |
| Interruption | split, missing segment, cut or offset at a score event |
| Polyphony | use independent grids/planes as literal coordinated voices |
| Tension / Release | intensify collision, then realign systems after a threshold |
| Tempo of Change | control distance between transformation events along a path |

## Grasshopper/Rhino modeling guideline

1. Create and preserve two or three source systems, such as structural grid, city/site
   grid, program bands or circulation vector field.
2. Establish protected zones for doors, egress, structure, minimum room envelope and
   sensitive facade conditions.
3. Apply transformations through a data table containing operation, target IDs,
   magnitude, pivot, priority and score binding.
4. Retain pre-transform geometry for every target and display before/after vectors.
5. Intersect transformed planes with host volumes, then rationalize boundary curves and
   remove sub-minimum fragments.
6. Separate expressive outer plates/screens from the continuous weather enclosure when
   the geometry requires it.
7. At MTA-F2, resolve seams where systems collide: folded return, open drained joint,
   glazed slot or nested layer. Each type receives a detail ID.
8. Run clashes after every operation batch and reject the batch atomically on hard
   program/structure/envelope failure.
9. Bake source systems, transformed elements and barrier/support layers separately.

## Tectonic compatibility

- **Frame — native/conditional.** Conflicting grids and displaced infill are legible;
  transferred or skewed loads need explicit limits.
- **Tensile — conditional.** Fragmented boundary frames and tensioned infill can work,
  while prestress/form-finding authority limits arbitrary displacement.
- **Shell — native/conditional.** Folded or split shells can express controlled rupture;
  surface continuity, force flow and fabrication require strong validation.

## Typology notes

- **Library:** use limited disruptions at public thresholds and circulation while reading
  zones retain clarity and comfort.
- **Theater:** front/back-of-house, city/foyer or performance/public grids can form source
  conflicts; egress and acoustic enclosure are hard protected zones.
- **Museum:** curatorial path, site trace and gallery grid can collide; gallery walls and
  daylight control remain typology-owned.

## Validation

- 100% of transformed elements resolve to source IDs and an operation record;
- protected-zone intersections equal zero;
- the continuous enclosure/barrier path can be traced through every collision detail;
- source systems remain visually recoverable in analytical drawings;
- residual fragments below material limits are absent or explicitly redesigned;
- score changes alter event magnitude/frequency only inside declared bounds;
- turning off transformations returns a valid baseline facade with matching IDs.

## Limitations

The grammar represents a common operational research lens across diverse architects. It
does not claim a unified historical doctrine. Apparent formal instability needs a much
deeper structural, envelope and safety workflow before construction claims are possible.
